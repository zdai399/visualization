# Data Visualization

## Assignment 3: Final Project

### Requirements:
- Dataset of interest:https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/licensed-dogs-and-cats/; Licensed Dogs and Cats
  The dataset include data on licensed dogs and cats residing in Toronto after year 2023.
    > Who is your intended audience? 
    My intended audience is the general public. 
    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 
    I want to show the most popular dog breeds in Toronto since year 2023 by counting the top 10 dog breeds with the most number of dogs from the licensed dogs dataset. 
    > What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 
    I want to make my plot easy to read so I ordered the bars by size from the biggest to smallest. 
    I aslo make the bars different color to show that each bar is a different breed.
    I also made sure to include detailed titles and axis labels so that the reader can understand the graph.
    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
    My data visualizations are reproducible because I did minimal data manipulation and the exact code for visualization is also provided. 
    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  
    I made sure my visualization is accessible by using a color palette that's soft to look at and for many people with colorblinded thay can tell apart the color chosen (tested by the color blindness simulator)
    and finally I don't solely rely on color and I also added texts in a sans-serif font for ease of reading. 
    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
    This visualization provides a summary of "popular dog breeds" in the city of Toronto. Potentially people who might be impacted would be pet owners, pet breeders, pet shelter, etc. 
    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
    I first made sure what information I wanted to show and excluded all the information that may cause the visualization to be too confusing or unreadable. 
    For example, I chose only the top 10 breeds although there are hundreads of breeds. It would not be effectice if I were to plot all breeds in one graph. 
    The dataset also contained postal code information but I also chose to exclude because I did not need to show geographical information, rather I just left it at the "City-of-Toronto" level.
    I also chose to focus on dogs and avoid confusing people with similar information for cat breeds. If needed, a separate graph should be made for information about cat breeds. 
    > What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
    Underwater labour that contributed to my final visualization includes: data collectors, coders who made the r plot packages and designers who made the color palette.
